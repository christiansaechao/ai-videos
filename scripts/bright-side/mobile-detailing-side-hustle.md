**Target length:** ~10–11 minutes (~1,600 words of narration)

**Structure:** Cold-open scenario hook → promise → 9 numbered segments → outro loop.

## 1. THE WHOLE BUSINESS FITS IN YOUR TRUNK (0:35–1:40)

**[VISUAL: Animated trunk opening; items pop in one by one with price tags.]**

**NARRATOR:**
Most businesses need a building, employees, and a scary bank loan. This one? It needs a parking spot.

Here's the entire starter kit: a bucket with a grit guard, car shampoo, microfiber towels, wheel cleaner, interior brushes, and a decent vacuum. Total damage? Around $150 to $250.

Compare that to a food truck — fifty thousand dollars. A franchise? Sometimes half a million.

And here's the part people miss: you don't even need water at first. Ever heard of rinseless wash? One bucket, one gallon, special solution. Detailers in apartments use it every single day.

Your customer provides the driveway. Sometimes the electricity too. You're basically renting your workspace... for free.

## 2. THE MATH IS ALMOST UNFAIR (1:40–2:50)

**[VISUAL: Animated calculator; numbers stack into a bar chart.]**

**NARRATOR:**
Okay, let's talk numbers — because this is where it gets fun.

A basic wash-and-vacuum goes for $50 to $80. A full interior-plus-exterior detail? $150 to $300, depending on your city. And each job takes two to four hours.

So do the math with me. Two full details on a Saturday... that's $400. Do that every weekend, and you've added over $1,600 a month — without touching your day job.

But what does it cost YOU per job? Soap, towels, a little product... maybe $10 to $15. That means you keep around 90 percent of every dollar.

Ninety. Percent. Margins.

Restaurants dream of 10 percent. You just tripled a waiter's shift... with a bucket.

## 3. DIRTY CARS ARE EVERYWHERE — LITERALLY (2:50–3:50)

**[VISUAL: Drone shot over packed parking lots; animated counter ticking up.]**

**NARRATOR:**
Look out your window. See all those cars? Every single one of them gets dirty. Rain or shine, recession or boom.

There are over 280 million registered vehicles in the United States alone. And the average driver? Hasn't deep-cleaned their interior in... well, ever.

Here's the psychology: people HATE cleaning their own car. It's right up there with doing taxes. But they LOVE a clean car. That gap between "hate doing it" and "love having it"? That gap is your paycheck.

And unlike dropshipping or crypto or whatever your cousin is into this month — this demand never goes away. Cars have been getting dirty for a hundred years. They're not stopping now.

## 4. YOU COME TO THEM — AND THEY PAY EXTRA FOR IT (3:50–4:50)

**[VISUAL: Split screen — person waiting bored at a car wash vs. person working on a laptop at home while their car gets detailed outside.]**

**NARRATOR:**
Quick question: why would someone pay YOU $200 when the car wash down the street charges $25?

One word: convenience.

Your customer never leaves their couch. They text you, go back to their meeting, and two hours later their car looks brand new — in their own driveway. You're not selling clean cars. You're selling time.

And busy people — realtors showing houses, parents with cheerio-covered back seats, business owners with client-facing vehicles — will happily pay double to get their Saturday back.

That's why "mobile" isn't just a detail... it IS the business model.

## 5. YOUR FIRST CUSTOMER IS CLOSER THAN YOU THINK (4:50–5:50)

**[VISUAL: Phone screen; texts going out to "Mom," "Coach Dave," "Neighbor Rita." Replies ping back.]**

**NARRATOR:**
"But I don't know anything about marketing!"

Good news — you don't need to. Your first ten customers are already in your phone.

Detail your own car. Take before-and-after photos — and make them dramatic. Post them everywhere: your story, the neighborhood Facebook group, Nextdoor. Then send one simple text to twenty people: "Starting a mobile detailing service. First five bookings get 20 percent off."

Here's the secret weapon though: the before-and-after photo. Nothing on the internet stops a scroll like a disgusting seat... turned spotless. It's oddly satisfying content — and it sells FOR you while you sleep.

One detailer's rule of thumb: every clean car parked in a driveway is a billboard the whole street sees.

## 6. THE UPSELLS ARE WHERE THE REAL MONEY HIDES (5:50–7:00)

**[VISUAL: Animated menu board; add-ons light up with prices.]**

**NARRATOR:**
Now — you've done the basic detail. Here's where beginners stop... and pros get rich.

Pet hair removal? That's a $40 add-on — and takes twenty extra minutes. Headlight restoration? Fifteen minutes of polishing, $50. Engine bay cleaning, odor removal, stain extraction — each one, $30 to $75 on top.

And the king of them all: ceramic coating. Materials cost you maybe $60... and customers pay $500 to $1,500 for it. Yes, you'll need practice first. But that's a thousand-dollar skill you can learn from free videos.

Suddenly that $150 job becomes a $350 job — same driveway, same afternoon.

By the way — if you're finding this useful, hit that like button. It genuinely helps more people discover the channel. Okay, back to the money.

## 7. IT'S RECESSION-RESISTANT (YES, REALLY) (7:00–8:00)

**[VISUAL: Animated line graph dipping, then a little car icon riding steadily across it.]**

**NARRATOR:**
Here's something counterintuitive. When the economy gets rough... detailing can actually get BUSIER.

Why? Think about it for a second.

When money's tight, people stop buying new cars — and start keeping their old ones longer. And a car you're keeping for eight years instead of three? That car needs maintenance. Protection. Care.

Meanwhile, used car values stay strong, and a professional detail can add hundreds to a resale price. Spending $200 to gain $800 on a sale isn't an expense — it's an investment. And your customers know it.

Boom times: people pamper their cars. Tough times: people protect their cars. Either way... your phone rings.

## 8. YOU LEARN REAL BUSINESS SKILLS — WITH TRAINING WHEELS (8:00–8:55)

**[VISUAL: Skill icons unlocking like a video game: pricing, scheduling, negotiation, reviews.]**

**NARRATOR:**
Here's the benefit nobody talks about.

Mobile detailing is basically a business degree that pays YOU tuition. Pricing, scheduling, customer service, handling complaints, asking for reviews, tracking expenses — you learn all of it, one driveway at a time.

Mess up a job? You lose an afternoon, not your life savings. That's the training-wheels advantage: the stakes are low, but the lessons are real.

And those skills transfer to ANY business you start later. Plenty of successful entrepreneurs started with a mop, a mower... or a microfiber towel.

## 9. THE SCALE-UP SECRET: STOP SELLING WASHES, START SELLING SUBSCRIPTIONS (8:55–10:10)

**[VISUAL: Calendar animation — one-off bookings transform into repeating monthly slots; revenue counter climbs.]**

**NARRATOR:**
And now — number 9. The one that changes everything.

The biggest mistake new detailers make? Treating every job as a one-time gig. The pros do something completely different: they sell maintenance plans.

Here's the pitch: "Instead of $200 twice a year, how about $89 a month — and your car NEVER gets dirty again?"

Now watch the math. Thirty clients on a $89 monthly plan... is $2,670 a month. Recurring. Predictable. Before a single new customer calls.

That's the moment this stops being a side hustle... and starts being a company. Next comes hiring your first helper, wrapping your car with your logo, landing small fleet contracts — realtors, delivery vans, dealerships with overflow lots.

There are solo detailers who turned this exact playbook into six-figure businesses in under three years. They didn't invent anything. They just... showed up, did great work, and made it repeatable.

And it all started with $200 and a driveway.
