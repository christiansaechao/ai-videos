# MURPH — Character Kit & Scripts (40)

The basset hound in the cubicle. Murph is the **audience surrogate** — the person
actually considering this stuff, doing the math at his desk, and deciding.

He's never the butt of the joke. The audience roots for him. His comedy is the gap
between the dream and his Tuesday.

**Why he carries value too:** Rocco lies, Buck corrects. Murph does something
different — he does the **real arithmetic out loud**, from the position of someone
who has to actually decide. That's the most persuasive form of the value.

## Murph's Format — "The Math"

| Shot | Role | Rule |
|---|---|---|
| **1 — THE PITCH** | Flatly repeats a hustle claim he just saw | Zero enthusiasm. He's quoting, not selling. |
| **2 — THE MATH** | Does the arithmetic out loud | **Real numbers. This is the payload.** |
| **3 — THE TRUTH** | What the math actually means for him | Where the honesty and the joke live |
| **4 — THE CLOSER** | Signature: **"...I'm gonna go back to work."** | Delivered totally flat |

**Signature elements:**
- Closer: **"...I'm gonna go back to work."** (Arc moments may vary it — see §Arc.)
- He never gets excited. Peak emotion = a slower blink.
- He often holds the chipped mug and never drinks from it.
- He does the math **correctly**. Murph is not stupid — he's tired.

**Look anchor:** basset hound, droopy jowls, wrinkled shirt, clip-on tie, crooked
lanyard, chipped mug.

## Murph's Arc — Why the Order Matters

Murph is the only character who **changes**. Rocco stays a fraud, Buck stays
competent — Murph moves, and that's what makes people follow the channel rather
than just watch a video.

| Range | Where he is | Closer |
|---|---|---|
| **1–3** | Pure skepticism. Doing math to prove it's fake. | "...I'm gonna go back to work." |
| **4–7** | The math keeps checking out. He's unsettled. | Starts varying — "...I'm gonna think about it." |
| **8–9** | Small actions. Saves a listing. Keeps a napkin. Cancels a unit. | "...I'm gonna leave this open." |
| **10** | He acts. | "...I'm gonna go find another one." |

**MU-CF-7 → MU-CF-10 is the payoff run** — parked outside a stranger's house, knocks,
buys the car, cleans it for six hours, sells it for a thousand dollars. Post those
four in order, close together. That's the emotional spine of the whole channel, and
it's the sequence that will convert viewers into followers.

**Never let Murph become a success story.** The moment he's rich he becomes Rocco.
He makes a thousand dollars, and his reaction is to go find another one — still
tired, still deadpan, still in the same beige apartment. That's the whole character.
