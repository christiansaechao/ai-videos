## 1. The Data, Distilled

- **The window is 1.7–3 seconds.** Average mobile viewing decision is 1.7s; 63% of
  highest-CTR videos hook within 3s. If the first frame doesn't communicate the payoff,
  the scroll happens before your voiceover finishes a sentence.
- **Visual hook + verbal hook, simultaneously** — not sequentially. A bold text overlay
  claim on screen *while* the voiceover says something different-but-complementary
  outperforms either alone. This matters most for screen-recording/demo content, which has
  no face to carry the hook.
- **Best length: 21–34 seconds** for the full ad; **15–20s** is the TikTok-native sweet
  spot when the algorithm is doing the work rather than paid delivery.
- **Top hook type = demonstration** (before/after or "watch this happen") — it out-performs
  the lowest-performing hook type by roughly 2x in views per clip.
- **Volume beats polish.** Brands shipping 30 variants/week beat brands shipping 3 —
  UGC-style content gets ~2.4x higher completion rates than polished brand video. This is
  the entire reason to use Kling instead of hiring a video team: for less than 2% of the
  cost of 30 human UGC videos, you get 30 AI variants in about a week, so you can hook-test
  like a performance marketer instead of guessing once.
- **Structure that converts:** Hook (makes a promise) → Body/Proof (demo or PAS: problem →
  agitate the cost of inaction → solve) → CTA (specific, low-friction, urgent). For app
  demos specifically, a real screen recording of an actual interaction (tap, scan, swipe)
  consistently beats a static mockup or narrated slide.

## 2. The Six Hook Types, Ranked, Mapped to ShowTheReceipts

Every hook below is written so the **first frame + first spoken word land inside 2
seconds**, per the data above.

### 1st — Demonstration hook (highest performer)
Show the transformation happening live, no explanation needed yet.

- **Text overlay:** *"watch this"* / **VO:** *"I'm about to photograph this crumpled
  flyer..."* → cut to full digital price list populating in real time.
  → **This is the Magic AI flyer scan.** It's your single best asset — data says
  demonstration hooks roughly double view counts, and this feature is inherently visual.
- Variant: dirty truck bed → time-lapse wash → sparkling reveal, on-screen text *"he got
  paid before he left the driveway"* → cut to Quick Charge receipt screen.

### 2nd — Bold claim / specific-number hook
Specific numbers read as credible; round numbers read as marketing.

- *"I built my entire price list in 47 seconds. From a photo of a napkin."*
- *"This app costs $15. The one I used to use cost $2,436 a year."*
- *"37 of the 50 founding spots are already gone."* (scarcity variant — pairs well with
  the July offer)

### 3rd — Problem-agitation hook (PAS opener)
Name the specific pain before you're even in frame.

- *"You're still typing 'Venmo me' into a text message after every job?"*
- *"If a client says 'I already paid you' and you can't prove it — that's $200 gone."*
  → pairs directly with the "Show The Receipts" brand payoff and paid-outside claim flow.
- *"Your Jobber bill this month was more than your last 3 jobs combined."*

### 4th — POV / relatability hook
Buyer sees themself in the scene. Works because it reads as advice, not a pitch — most
native-feeling format for local-service audiences.

- *"POV: a client asks if you take card on-site and you say 'yeah, one sec' instead of
  'uh, only cash.'"*
- *"POV: you finally stop doing invoices from your car at 9pm."*
- *"POV: you just found out the software you've been paying $130/month for has a $15
  version built for exactly you."*

### 5th — Question hook
Opens a curiosity gap; works best paired with an unexpected or contrarian answer.

- *"Why does a one-person detailing business need the same software as a 40-truck HVAC
  company?"* (sets up the Jobber/ServiceTitan wedge directly)
- *"What if your flyer WAS your website?"*

### 6th — Founder/UGC testimonial hook
Lower raw view counts than demonstration hooks but highest trust — best used for retargeting
warm audiences (people who already saw a demo hook) and for the build-in-public thread.

- *"I'm building software for solo detailers because I watched one lose a $400 job to a
  scheduling mix-up."*
- *"Day 12: 31 founding operators. Here's the one feature that's converting all of them."*

**Priority for July:** lead with **Demonstration** and **Problem-agitation** hooks (cheapest
to produce with Kling, highest raw reach); layer **Founder** hooks in weeks 3–4 once there's
real traction to reference.

## 3. Demo Video Body Structures (the 15–34s after the hook)

### A. The 6-Shot UGC Ad (best for cold-traffic Kling variants)
`Hook → Problem → Product Reveal → Proof → Endorsement → CTA`

Applied to Magic AI flyer scan:
1. **Hook (0–2s):** Crumpled paper flyer in hand, text overlay "watch this"
2. **Problem (2–6s):** Quick cut — person hunched over laptop manually typing prices,
   visibly annoyed. VO: *"I used to spend my Sunday nights building my price list by hand."*
3. **Product reveal (6–14s):** Screen recording — open app, tap scan, photograph the flyer,
   watch the catalog populate (tiers, add-ons, pricing) in real time
4. **Proof (14–20s):** Text overlay with the actual number: *"60 seconds. Zero typing."*
5. **Endorsement (20–26s):** Founder or operator on camera, single line: *"This is the
   only reason I switched."*
6. **CTA (26–30s):** *"Link in bio. $15/month. First 50 people get it for $9 forever."*

### B. Problem-Agitate-Solve (best for problem-agitation hooks)
`Name the pain (specific) → Cost of inaction (concrete $ or time) → Solve (demo, 8–12s) → CTA`

Applied to Quick Charge / getting paid on-site:
- **Problem:** *"You finish the job and the client says 'let me Venmo you later.'"*
- **Agitate:** *"Later becomes never for about 1 in 10 jobs. That's real money."*
- **Solve (screen recording):** tap Quick Charge → tap the preset service → client taps
  Apple Pay on their own phone → receipt auto-sends. All in one continuous screen capture.
- **CTA:** *"Get paid before you leave the driveway. Link in bio."*

### C. Before/After (best for demonstration hooks, no dialogue needed)
`Chaotic "before" state (3–5s) → fast cut/whip-pan transition → clean "after" state (3–5s) → text-only CTA`

Applied to invoicing: messy handwritten estimate on a clipboard → whip-pan → same estimate
as a branded, professional PDF invoice on a phone screen. Zero VO required — pure visual,
captioned, sound-on trending audio. Cheapest possible Kling variant to produce at volume.

### D. POV Micro-Story (best for POV hooks, 15–20s max)
`POV hook (2s) → one beat of the scenario played out (8–10s) → payoff line + CTA (4–5s)`

No product screen needed at all in some variants — pure relatability, product mentioned
only in caption/CTA overlay. These are your highest-share, lowest-direct-conversion videos;
run them for reach, not for the sale.

## 4. Kling Production Notes (multi-shot workflow)

Kling 3.0's multi-shot mode stitches several "scene" prompts into one continuous video —
this is what makes the 6-shot structure in §3A producible in a single generation rather than
six separate clips.

**Per-shot prompt pattern** (documentary/UGC realism, not glossy ad look):
```
[Shot N, duration]: [camera move — static / slow push-in / whip-pan / handheld],
[subject + wardrobe + setting], [lighting: natural/warm, "iPhone-shot" grain],
[action beat], [Speaker: role, tone]: "[exact line]"
```

**Working defaults for ShowTheReceipts UGC:**
- **Look:** handheld or tripod "iPhone footage" realism, natural daylight or warm indoor
  light — NOT cinematic/glossy. Polished = ad-brain = scroll-past. UGC-style completion
  rates are ~2.4x higher than polished brand video.
- **Cast:** rotate between "solo operator" personas — detailer in driveway/garage, pressure
  washer on a job site, founder at a kitchen-table desk. Match wardrobe/setting to the
  niche the video targets (detailing vs. pressure washing).
- **Screen-recording shots:** for product-reveal beats, composite a real captured screen
  recording of the actual app rather than generating the UI in Kling — authenticity of the
  real interaction (tap, scan, swipe) is the entire point and AI-generated UI reads as fake.
- **Negative prompt baseline:** `jittery eyes, frozen lips, character drift, plastic skin,
  over-lit, studio look, stock-photo smile`
- **Captions:** burn in bold, high-contrast captions on every video — sound-off viewing is
  the majority case on TikTok.
- **Export:** 1080×1920, 9:16, captions burned in, 15–30s.

**Volume cadence:** write 5 hook lines per week (rotate through the 6 types above), generate
3–5 Kling variants per hook = 15–25 variants/week. Post the top 3–5 performers per week to
the main accounts; treat the rest as organic A/B tests. This is the "30 variants/week beats
3" principle from the research — cheap enough with Kling that testing volume itself becomes
the strategy, not a nice-to-have.

## 6. What to Track

Per the existing strategy's KPI section — add hook-level granularity:
- **3-second view-through rate per hook type** (TikTok analytics) — this tells you which
  of the 6 hook types is actually working for *this* audience, not just what the general
  research says
- **Completion rate by structure** (6-shot vs. PAS vs. before/after vs. POV)
- Kill any hook type under median 3s-VTR after 5 variants; double down on whichever
  type/feature pairing is winning by end of week 2, and shift the week 3–4 slate toward it
